About

RELEASED

Paste a link, pick a format, download. Video or music from YouTube and more than a thousand other sites.

Queenfisher is a window around yt-dlp, the downloader that keeps up with the sites. It shows you what a video really offers before you download it: the picture, the length, the qualities it has and how big the file will be. Queue up as many as you like and they come down one after another.

  • 14 formats: MP4, MKV, WebM, MOV, AVI, FLV and GIF, or MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG, M4A, ALAC and the original sound
  • YouTube previews show up almost at once
  • Subtitles, whole playlists, and skipping sponsor segments
  • Keeps yt-dlp up to date by itself and checks the developers' signature first
  • No ads, no accounts, no tracking
Runs on:
Windows 10/11, macOS, Linux

Guide

Click a topic to open it.

Install on Windows
  1. Click Download for Windows.
  2. If Edge holds it ("not commonly downloaded"): ... menu, then Keep, then Keep anyway.
  3. Open Queenfisher.exe. If Windows says it protected your PC: More info, then Run anyway.
  4. Put it wherever you like; it's the whole program.

The first time, it fetches yt-dlp, ffmpeg and Deno by itself (about 150 MB) and keeps them up to date.

Install on macOS
  1. Download for Apple Silicon (M1 and newer) or Intel.
  2. Open the zip and move Queenfisher to Applications.
  3. The first time, macOS blocks it: click Done, then System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway (near the bottom).
  4. Install ffmpeg: brew install ffmpeg
Install on Linux
  1. Download the AppImage.
  2. Make it executable: chmod +x Queenfisher-Linux.AppImage, then run it.
  3. Install ffmpeg from your package manager.
Download a video
  1. Copy the video's link and click Paste (or press Ctrl+Shift+V).
  2. Pick a format and a quality on the left. Qualities the video doesn't have are greyed out.
  3. Click Download. Add more while it runs; they wait their turn.

Files go to your Downloads folder. Change... next to "Save to" picks another one, and Queenfisher remembers it.

Which format should I pick?
MP4:
plays everywhere. Up to 1080p on YouTube.
MKV, WebM:
the best quality the site has, 1440p and 4K included.
MOV:
for video editors and Apple devices.
AVI, FLV:
for old players. AVI is converted, which takes a while.
GIF:
short clips up to a minute, no sound.
MP3, M4A:
music for any player or phone.
WAV, FLAC, ALAC:
for editing or music libraries. Bigger files, same sound.
Original:
the site's own sound as it is, nothing converted.
Extras: subtitles, playlists, sponsors
Subtitles:
English subtitles inside the video, when the site has them (MP4, MKV, WebM).
Whole playlist:
a link from a playlist downloads every video in it.
Skip sponsors:
cuts sponsored segments out of YouTube videos, using the SponsorBlock community's list.
Downloading the same video again

Another quality is saved as its own file. The same choice again asks whether to keep the file you have or overwrite it; the old file stays until the new one is complete.

Keeping yt-dlp working

Sites change all the time, and yt-dlp keeps up. Queenfisher fetches official yt-dlp releases, checks the developers' signature, and looks for a new one once a day. When a download fails because a site changed, it updates yt-dlp and tries again by itself.

If something goes wrong
  • The Status column says what happened. Log, under the downloads, shows the details.
  • Select a failed download and click Retry.
  • On Linux, a video that needs a sign-in uses your browser's, once you're signed in there.
  • Still stuck? Open an issue on GitHub.
